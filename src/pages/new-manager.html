Stepping up to manage

Managing people for the first time? Here's how to start well.

Being good at the work is why you got the job, and it is not what the job is now. The first year as a manager sets habits that last, so it is worth starting deliberately. Nobody is born knowing how to run a one-to-one.

Your first 90 days

Stepping up to manager is a change of job, not a bigger version of the old one. Use your first 90 days as a manager to learn the team before you try to improve it.

  • Meet everyone one to one before you change anything. Ask what is working, what is not, and what they need from you. Listen more than you talk.
  • Find out what your manager actually expects from you. Ask what success looks like at six months, and get it in writing if you can, even if that is your own email summing up the conversation.
  • Decide what you will stop doing yourself. Write the list. Hand over anything someone in the team could do, with care, and then leave it alone.
  • Set a weekly rhythm. A regular one-to-one with each person, a short team check-in, and an hour a week to think about the team rather than the work.
  • Pick one thing to be known for. Clear priorities, quick decisions, honest feedback: choose one, and be consistent about it before you add a second.

The things nobody tells you

  • Your old peers are now your team, and it is awkward for them too. Name it early, in private, and agree how you will work together now.
  • You will be slower at the work and faster at the job. It feels like going backwards. It isn't. You are measured on what the team delivers now, not on what you deliver yourself.
  • Feedback is the whole job. Small, specific and often beats one big conversation twice a year, for praise as much as for problems.
  • You will be managed harder, not softer. Your own manager expects more from you now, and will notice what your team does when you are not in the room.

Where new managers come unstuck

  • Doing the work instead of running it. It is the comfortable option, and it leaves the team waiting for you.
  • Avoiding the difficult conversation. It does not get easier with time. It gets more expensive.
  • Copying the last manager they had. Keep what worked for you, and remember your team are not you.
  • Waiting to feel ready. Most managers feel ready some time after they needed to be. Act anyway, and adjust as you go.

What not to do

  • Change everything in the first month.
  • Keep doing your old job as well as the new one.
  • Stay everyone's friend at the cost of being clear.
  • Save your feedback for the annual review.
  • Pretend you have it all worked out.
