Landing the next role

Ready for your next role? Work out what it is before you start applying.

Most people start with the job boards and end up with a CV that could be anyone's. The order that works is the other way round: what you want, then your story, then the search. A job search is a project. Most people run it like a lottery.

Before you look

  • What you want more of, and less of. Look back over the last few years and be specific: the work, the people, the pace.
  • The three things the next role must have. Three, not ten. These are the ones you won't trade away.
  • What you would say no to. A salary floor, a commute, a kind of culture. Decide now, while nobody is offering you anything.
  • The 15 minute version. The Values Finder is a quick way to put words to what matters to you before you write a word of your CV.

Your story

  • The two-line answer to "what are you looking for?" If you can't say it in two lines, nobody can help you find it.
  • Your CV and LinkedIn are the same story in two formats. Write the story first, then fit each format to it, not the other way round.
  • The three examples you will use in every interview. Pick three pieces of work that show what you want to be hired for next, and know them well enough to tell each in two minutes.

The search

  • Your network before the boards. Tell people your two lines, and ask each of them who else you should talk to.
  • A short list of ten organisations, not a hundred applications. Learn them properly, and find someone inside each one.
  • A weekly rhythm and a stopping rule. Set the hours you will put in each week, and decide in advance when you will step back and change approach if it is not working.

Interviews and the offer

  • Prepare the questions you will ask, not only the ones you will answer. Good questions show judgement, and the answers tell you whether you want the job.
  • Buy a day to think. "Thank you, I'm keen. Can I come back to you by Thursday?" is a normal thing to say to an offer.
  • Check before you say yes. The role, the manager, the package in writing, and the first year's priorities. Ask anything you would regret not asking.

What not to do

  • Start with the job boards.
  • Send the same CV to every role.
  • Apply for everything that is roughly a fit.
  • Leave your network until the applications stop working.
  • Accept on the call before you have seen the offer in writing.
