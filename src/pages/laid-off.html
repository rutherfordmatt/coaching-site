After a layoff

Just been laid off? Here's what to do this week, and what can wait.

Being laid off is a shock, and it is survivable. Most of what you're feeling right now, from anger to numbness to the odd flash of relief, is normal, and you don't need to decide anything today. I was made redundant myself in 2026, so this isn't theory.

The first 72 hours

  • Don't sign anything today. Whatever you've been handed can wait until you've read it properly and slept on it.
  • Get everything in writing. If something was said in the meeting, ask for it in an email.
  • Note the dates. Your notice period, your last day and when your final payment lands. Put them somewhere you'll see them.
  • Decide who needs to know, and tell them on your terms. The people closest to you first. Everyone else can wait until you know what you want to say.
  • Take one day off the problem. Not to pretend it hasn't happened, but because you'll think more clearly afterwards.

The first two weeks

  • Read the package properly before you sign. If any figure, date or clause is unclear, get it checked by someone qualified to check it.
  • Ask for a written reference now. People are at their most generous in the first few weeks, and a few lines in writing now saves chasing later.
  • Line up two or three people who will say good things about you. Ask them now, tell them what you're aiming for, and keep them posted.
  • Money first, job search second. Work out your runway: what's coming in, what's going out, and how many months that gives you. Know that number before you apply for anything.
  • Decide what you'll say, and keep it to two lines. Something like: "My role was made redundant in a restructure. I'm taking a few weeks to work out the right next move."

What you're entitled to

What you're owed depends on where you work and what your contract says, so I won't try to summarise it here. The official sources are clear and kept up to date: Citizens Information for Ireland and GOV.UK for the UK. Anywhere else, start with your national employment authority.

What not to do

  • Fire off applications in week one.
  • Take the first thing offered out of panic.
  • Disappear from your network.
  • Rewrite your CV before you know what you want.
  • Treat this as a verdict on you. It was a decision about a role.
